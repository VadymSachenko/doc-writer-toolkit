# Verbs and voice

- Prefer active voice when the actor matters or improves clarity.
- Use passive voice when the action or result matters more, the actor is unknown, or
  naming the actor would distract.
- Use the imperative for steps, instructions, and direct calls to action.
- Use present tense for current product behavior and timeless facts.
- Use future tense only for a genuinely future event, not routine system response.
- Choose a strong verb instead of a weak verb plus abstract noun.
- Remove unnecessary *you can* when a direct verb states the capability accurately.
- Use *will* only for certainty; use *might* or *can* for possibility or capability.
- Clarify `-ing` words whose role could be read as a noun, adjective, or verb.
- Keep auxiliary verbs where omission would create a fragment or translation ambiguity.
