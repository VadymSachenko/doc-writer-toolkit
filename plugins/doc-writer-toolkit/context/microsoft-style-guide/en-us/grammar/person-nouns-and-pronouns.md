# Person, nouns, and pronouns

- Address the reader as *you* when giving guidance or explaining a direct consequence.
- Avoid switching between *you*, *the user*, *customer*, and other labels for the same
  person without a reason.
- Use *we* only when it clearly means Microsoft or the organization speaking and that
  ownership matters. Do not use *we* to mean the reader and writer together vaguely.
- Avoid first-person singular in product UI except where ownership must be distinguished
  in a shared space.
- Use singular *they* for a generic person and a real person's stated pronouns.
- Make every pronoun's antecedent unmistakable.
- Prefer concrete nouns. Avoid long strings of noun modifiers and unnecessary nominalizations.
- Capitalize proper nouns; lowercase ordinary role, feature, and object names unless the
  official name requires capitals.
