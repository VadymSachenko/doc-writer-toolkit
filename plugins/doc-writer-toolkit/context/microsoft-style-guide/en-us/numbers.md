# Numbers

- Match UI and literal examples exactly. Otherwise, spell out zero through nine and use
  numerals for 10 and greater.
- Use numerals for measurements, dimensions, values to enter, percentages, time, table
  coordinates, numbered sections, and round quantities of 1 million or more.
- If comparable items in one context include 10 or greater, use numerals for all of them.
- Avoid starting a sentence with a numeral; rewrite or spell it out.
- Use a leading zero for a decimal below one, except when showing input that omits it.
- Use a true minus sign for negative numbers, not an en dash.
- Use a multiplication sign with spaces for screen resolution, paper size, and similar dimensions.
- Use the percent sign only when the product or project convention requires it; otherwise
  Microsoft prose generally pairs a numeral with *percent*.
- Spell month names in English dates to avoid numeric ambiguity. Do not apply English
  date ordering to Ukrainian.
- Prefer *from X through Y* in prose; use an en dash for compact ranges and *to* for time ranges.
- Avoid `K`, `M`, and `B` for thousand, million, and billion unless space requires them.
