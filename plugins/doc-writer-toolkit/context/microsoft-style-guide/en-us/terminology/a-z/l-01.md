# Microsoft terms: L (1)

Load this file only when checking one of the indexed terms below.

<a id="like"></a>
## -like

- In general, don't hyphenate words ending with -like unless the root word ends in double l' s or the root word has three or more syllables.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/like)

<a id="labeled-labeling"></a>
## labeled, labeling

- Prefer one l, not two.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/labeled-labeling)

## laptop

- Follow the referenced topic collection for this term.
- Related guidance: Computer and device term collection.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/laptop)

<a id="later"></a>
## later

- If possible, use a hyperlink to help readers find information elsewhere in the content.
- If you can't use a hyperlink, use later instead of below in cross-references such as later in this article.
- Acceptable: or later to refer to multiple versions of software.
- Keep in mind that the phrases and later and or later might imply that the functionality or feature discussed will be included or supported in all future releases.
- If you're not sure, list each applicable version instead.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/later)

<a id="launch"></a>
## launch

- Avoid this term when you mean opening an app or form.
- Prefer open instead.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/launch)

<a id="lay-out-laid-out-layout"></a>
## lay out, laid out, layout

- In formatting discussions:
- Prefer lay out as a verb to describe positioning elements on a page. - Use laid out as a verb in the past tense. - Use layout to describe the result of laying out…
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/lay-out-laid-out-layout)

<a id="leading"></a>
## leading

- Prefer instead of preceding or beginning in phrases such as leading slashes or leading spaces, which might occur at the beginning of strings, parameters, or other values. Leading in this context is the opposite…

<a id="leave"></a>
## leave

- Avoid to refer to closing an app or program.
- Prefer close instead.
- Related guidance: Describing interactions with UI.

<a id="left-align-left-aligned"></a>
## left align, left-aligned

- Prefer to refer to text that's aligned at the left margin.
- Write with a hyphen left-aligned.
- Avoid left-justified.
- Related guidance: justify, justified, right align, right-aligned.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/left-align-left-aligned)

<a id="left-justify-left-justified"></a>
## left justify, left-justified

- Avoid.
- Prefer left-aligned instead.
- Related guidance: left align, left-aligned.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/left-justify-left-justified)

<a id="left-leftmost-left-hand"></a>
## left, leftmost, left-hand

- Avoid left as a directional term by itself.
- Instead, use a term such as upper left, lower left, leftmost, or on the left side of.
- Include a hyphen when you use left to modify a noun, as in upper-left corner.
- Avoid left-hand as a modifier.
- Accessibility tip Don't use directional terms (left, right, up, down) as the only clue to location.
- Related guidance: a change in the interface as.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/left-leftmost-left-hand)

<a id="legacy"></a>
## legacy

- Avoid to describe a previous version of a product.
- Prefer previous, former, earlier, or a similar term instead.
- Be specific if necessary, especially when discussing compatibility issues.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/legacy)

<a id="legal"></a>
## legal

- Prefer only to refer to matters of law.
- Avoid this term when you mean valid, as in a valid action.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/legal)

<a id="less-vs-fewer-vs-under"></a>
## less vs. fewer vs. under

- When comparing quantities:
- Prefer less to refer to a mass amount, value, or degree. - Use fewer to refer to a countable number of items. - Don't use under to refer to a quantity or number.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/less-vs-fewer-vs-under)

<a id="let-lets"></a>
## let, lets

- Avoid lets (or allows or enables) to refer to things that Microsoft or Microsoft apps make possible for the customer.
- Instead, focus on what the customer wants to do.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/let-lets)

<a id="leverage"></a>
## leverage

- Avoid this term as a verb to mean take advantage of. Use take advantage of, use, or another more appropriate word or phrase.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/leverage)

<a id="line"></a>
## line

- Avoid to refer to a series of related Microsoft products or services.
- Prefer family instead.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/line)

<a id="line-of-business-line-of-business"></a>
## line of business, line-of-business

- Write in lowercase.
- Don’t hyphenate as a noun.
- Write with a hyphen as an adjective.
- Avoid abbreviating as LOB.
- Source: [Microsoft Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/a-z-word-list-term-collections/l/line-of-business)
