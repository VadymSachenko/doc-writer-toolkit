# Global-ready writing

- Assume that readers may use another first language and that content may be translated.
- Use short sentences with ordinary subject–verb–object order when natural.
- Split long clause chains; use lists or tables for genuinely parallel information.
- Include articles, relative pronouns, and verbs when they clarify grammatical roles.
- Avoid idioms, puns, colloquialisms, culture-specific metaphors, and unexplained slang.
- Avoid long stacks of modifiers. Put modifiers next to what they modify.
- Prefer active voice and indicative mood; use the imperative for procedures.
- Use one term for one concept and do not vary terminology merely for style.
- Clarify ambiguous `-ing` and `-ed` forms in English with explicit syntax or a rewrite.
- Limit unexplained abbreviations to forms established for the audience and locale.
- Do not make English fragments so compressed that translation depends on guesswork.

Localization adapts content to a locale; it is not a word-for-word substitution. Apply
the target language's grammar, cultural conventions, legal constraints, and approved
terminology.
