# Global examples, art, names, time, and currency

## Examples and images

- Prefer generic, culturally neutral scenarios and vary locations and identities.
- Avoid assuming US laws, paper sizes, sports, holidays, seasons, standards, family
  structures, or business customs are universal.
- Avoid hand signs, disputed maps, protected landmarks, flag-like color combinations,
  and art based on English idioms without local review.
- Keep editable text out of images when practical; provide captions and alt text.
- Consider bandwidth and device limits when choosing animation and large images.

## Forms and personal data

- Prefer **Full name**, or **First name** and **Last name** when the distinction is
  required. Make middle name and title optional.
- Allow names, addresses, phone numbers, and postal codes to vary in length and format.
- Use **State or province**, **Country or region**, and **Postal code** for global forms.
- Do not assume that addressing a person by name is appropriate in every locale.
- Collect only information necessary for the task.

## Dates, time, seasons, places, and money

- Format dates, numbers, units, currency, and addresses according to the output locale.
- Include the time zone when readers could otherwise interpret an event differently.
- Avoid seasons; use months or quarters. If a season is essential, identify the hemisphere.
- Include the country or region for event locations when the audience is international.
- Use an unambiguous currency code when the symbol alone could refer to several currencies.
- Do not apply the English guide's `month dd, yyyy` form to Ukrainian content.
