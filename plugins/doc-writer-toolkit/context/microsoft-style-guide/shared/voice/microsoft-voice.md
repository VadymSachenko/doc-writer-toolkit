# Microsoft voice and top principles

## Load this file when

Use it for any drafting or editing task that must sound like Microsoft.

## Voice

- Be warm and relaxed: natural, conversational, and appropriately friendly.
- Be crisp and clear: front-load the point and write for scanning.
- Be ready to help: anticipate the reader's need and make the next action obvious.
- Keep the voice consistent, but adjust tone to the situation and the reader's state.
- Prefer a few strong ideas to many weak or repetitive statements.

## Rules

### MWSG-VOICE-001 — Lead with the outcome

**Strength:** Required  
**Rule:** Put the takeaway, choice, warning, or next action before background details.

**Recommended:** Save time by reusing a project template.  
**Not recommended:** A project template is a file that contains settings that may
be reused, and it can help save time.

### MWSG-VOICE-002 — Write like a person

**Strength:** Required  
**Rule:** Use familiar words and natural sentence patterns. Read the text aloud;
revise jargon, robotic fragments, and unnecessarily technical phrasing.

**Recommended:** Enter an email address such as `name@example.com`.  
**Not recommended:** Invalid identifier.

### MWSG-VOICE-003 — Be brief without omitting necessary information

**Strength:** Required  
**Rule:** Give readers enough information to decide or act confidently, then stop.
Remove throat-clearing, repeated context, empty qualifiers, and obvious statements.

### MWSG-VOICE-004 — Use active, direct wording

**Strength:** Recommended  
**Rule:** Start actionable statements with a strong verb. Remove unnecessary
constructions such as *you can* and weak openings such as *there is*.

**Recommended:** Store, share, and open files from any device.  
**Not recommended:** You can store files, and there is also an option that lets you
share and open them from different devices.

### MWSG-VOICE-005 — Use sentence-style capitalization

Capitalize the first word and proper nouns. Do not use title case merely for emphasis.

### MWSG-VOICE-006 — Adapt tone to context

- Be calm and direct for errors, security, safety, and interruptions.
- Be empathetic when the reader may be blocked or frustrated.
- Use lightness only when it fits the moment; never make the reader or problem the joke.
- Use technical vocabulary with expert readers and plain equivalents with general readers.

## English-specific quick checks

For English content, also use contractions when natural, the serial comma, one space
after sentence-ending punctuation, and the punctuation rules for headings and lists.
