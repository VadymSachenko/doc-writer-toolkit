# Bias-free and inclusive communication

## People and pronouns

- Use gender-neutral role names and generic terms.
- For a generic person, prefer second person, a plural construction, a role, or singular
  *they*. Avoid paired forms such as *he/she*.
- For a real person, use that person's stated name and pronouns.
- Use gendered language only when gender is relevant, part of a quotation, or requested
  by the person concerned.

## Representation

- Use varied, realistic names, roles, families, abilities, locations, and circumstances.
- Do not assign roles or behavior according to stereotypes.
- Do not generalize about a culture, nationality, race, region, gender, age, ability,
  socioeconomic group, or other identity—even positively.
- Use geographically equivalent examples and avoid disputed political labels unless the
  subject requires them and they are sourced appropriately.

## Respectful wording

- Avoid slurs, profanity, demeaning slang, cultural appropriation, and language that
  trivializes violence, illness, disability, or historical harm.
- Replace unnecessarily militaristic metaphors with precise task language.
- Focus on people rather than portraying a disability as tragedy or suffering.
- Do not mention identity characteristics that have no bearing on the subject.
- Use approved, current community terminology and recheck terms that change over time.

**Recommended:** The customer can reset their password.  
**Not recommended:** The customer can reset his password.

**Recommended:** primary and replica  
**Not recommended:** master and slave
