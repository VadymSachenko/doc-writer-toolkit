# Write for a global audience

- Use US English, but write so that readers and translators do not need US cultural
  knowledge.
- Prefer short sentences, simple verbs, active voice, present tense, and common word
  order.
- Avoid phrasal verbs when a single clear verb works: prefer **start** to **spin up**.
- Keep modifiers next to what they modify. Avoid long noun stacks and misplaced
  **only**.
- Use a word in its primary, literal sense. Avoid idioms, colloquialisms, jokes,
  seasonal references, and culture-specific metaphors.
- Include helper words—articles, relative pronouns, and **then**—when they remove
  ambiguity. In sequences, **and then** is often clearer than **then** alone.
- Make every pronoun’s antecedent unmistakable. Define unfamiliar abbreviations.
- Address the reader directly; do not shift unpredictably among **you**, **we**, and
  third person.
- Keep terminology, sentence patterns, capitalization, and formatting consistent.
  Consistency improves comprehension and translation quality.
- Use apostrophes only for possession and contractions, not to form plurals.
- Use internationally understandable dates, units, names, and examples.

Source: Google Developer Documentation Style Guide, “Write for a global audience.”
