# Precision, claims, sources, and timelessness

## Claims

- State verifiable facts. Avoid subjective superlatives and absolute claims about
  performance, cost, reliability, simplicity, or security.
- Explain intended benefits or design properties instead of promising outcomes that
  depend on configuration, workload, threats, or future conditions.
- Support a measurable comparison with current evidence and enough context to
  interpret it.

## Future features

- Document available behavior, not unannounced or planned functionality.
- Do not pre-announce a feature in product documentation without the required legal
  and release approval.
- Prefer present tense for current behavior.

## Timeless documentation

- Avoid relative labels such as **new**, **latest**, **currently**, **recently**, or
  **soon** when the statement should remain valid.
- Describe the product state directly. Use a dated version or release note when time
  is materially relevant.
- Do not assume the reader remembers a previous interface or product behavior.

## Third-party material

- Do not copy third-party prose, images, code, logos, or speech merely because it is
  useful. Paraphrase the necessary information and link to the authoritative source.
- Quote only when the exact wording is necessary and permission or licensing permits
  it. Preserve attribution and license requirements.
