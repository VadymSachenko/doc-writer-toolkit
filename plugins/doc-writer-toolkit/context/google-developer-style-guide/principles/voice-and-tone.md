# Voice and tone

## Core voice

- Write conversationally, professionally, and respectfully.
- Be friendly without becoming frivolous, cute, or overly casual.
- Use familiar, precise words and direct sentence structures.
- Address the reader as **you**; use an imperative for instructions.
- Prefer active voice and present tense.
- Put the reader’s task and needed information before product promotion or writer
  personality.
- Use consistent language for a global audience with varied English proficiency.

## GDSG-VOICE-001 — Be direct

**Strength:** Required  
**Rule:** State the point, condition, or action without an ornamental introduction.

**Recommended:** To restrict access, update the policy.  
**Not recommended:** In order to take advantage of enhanced restrictions, you may
want to consider updating the policy.

## GDSG-VOICE-002 — Use literal language

Avoid clichés, slang, figurative language, culturally specific references, and humor
that readers or translators can misinterpret. Prefer a term’s primary meaning.

## GDSG-VOICE-003 — Be polite without adding *please*

Write respectful instructions, but normally omit **please**. A clear imperative is not
impolite in technical documentation.

**Recommended:** Select **Save**.  
**Not recommended:** Please select **Save**.

## GDSG-VOICE-004 — Keep emphasis rare

Avoid promotional enthusiasm, repeated intensifiers, and exclamation points. Use an
exclamation point only for a rare, genuinely exclamatory statement—not to make an
ordinary instruction or product claim sound exciting.

## Reader-first exception

These are guidelines rather than inflexible rules. A project convention may override
them when it clearly improves accuracy or reader success; apply the deviation
consistently.
