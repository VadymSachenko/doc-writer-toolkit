<a id="term-tap"></a>
## `tap`

**Scope:** `general, android-only`  
**Rule:** In Android documentation, use for on-screen and soft (capacitive) buttons. Use instead of click when the environment is definitely a touch device. Use instead of touch. However, touch & hold (not touch and hold) is Acceptable. For mechanical buttons, use press.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#tap)

<a id="term-tap-and-hold"></a>
## `tap & hold, tap and hold`

**Scope:** `do-not-use, android-only`  
**Rule:** In Android documentation, do not use. Prefer touch & hold. (Not touch and hold.)

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#tap-and-hold)

<a id="term-target"></a>
## `target`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Avoid using as a verb when possible, especially in reference to people. For some readers, target has aggressive connotations. Instead of "targeting" audiences, we try to attract them or appeal to them or make their lives easier. Acceptable to use target as an adjective, as in target audience, but consider rephrasing for clarity. Alternatives include phrases such as intended for, looking for, focused on, and interacting with.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#target)

<a id="term-terminate"></a>
## `terminate`

**Scope:** `use-with-caution`  
**Rule:** Avoid using as a synonym for stop. Prefer words like stop, exit, cancel, or end. For a specific context where you can use terminate as a synonym for stop, see Documenting command-line syntax. In some contexts, such as telephony and networking, terminate has specific technical meanings that aren't synonyms for stop; in those contexts, you can use terminate.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#terminate)

<a id="term-textbox"></a>
## `text box, textbox`

**Scope:** `do-not-use`  
**Rule:** Do not use. Prefer box. In Google Cloud documentation, use field instead of box. For example, "In the Instance field, specify a value less than 64 characters long." In Google Workspace documentation, use field instead of box. For example, "In the Instance field, specify a value less than 64 characters long."

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#textbox)

<a id="term-they"></a>
## `they (singular)`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** This is our preferred gender-neutral pronoun. Whether used as singular or plural, it always takes the plural verb. For example, "A user authenticates their identity by entering their password."

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#they)

<a id="term-third-party"></a>
## `third party (noun), third-party (adjective)`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Spell as third party when used as a noun and as third-party when used as an adjective. Avoid abbreviating to 3rd party or 3rd-party.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#third-party)

<a id="term-this-that"></a>
## `this, that`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Where possible, put a noun after this or that for clarity. If doing so results in clunky prose, then Do not do it; but even then, try thinking about what the noun would be. If you aren't sure what noun this or that refers to, then consider rephrasing—otherwise, your reader probably won't know what noun you're referring to, either.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#this-that)

<a id="term-time-frame"></a>
## `timeframe`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Use the heading form; do not use time frame. Avoid where possible, or use an alternative such as period, schedule, deadline, or when. But if you do use it, then write it as one word.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#time-frame)
