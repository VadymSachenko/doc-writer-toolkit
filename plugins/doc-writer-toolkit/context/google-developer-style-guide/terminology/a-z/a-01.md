# A terminology — 1 of 3

Scope labels are cumulative. `general` means no product-only marker was
present; a project-specific rule can still override this entry.

<a id="term-a-an"></a>
## `a and an`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Use a when the next word starts with a consonant sound, regardless of what letter it starts with.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#a-an)

<a id="term-ab"></a>
## `A/B testing`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Capitalize and use slash notation for A/B.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#ab)

<a id="term-abnormal"></a>
## `abnormal`

**Scope:** `use-with-caution`  
**Rule:** Do not use to refer to a person. Acceptable to refer to a condition of a computer system.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#abnormal)

<a id="term-abort"></a>
## `abort`

**Scope:** `use-with-caution`  
**Rule:** Avoid in general usage. Prefer words like stop, exit, cancel, or end. In Linux, abort refers to a type of signal that terminates an abnormal process.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#abort)

<a id="term-about-on"></a>
## `about versus on`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** When a cross-reference includes information that describes what the cross-reference links to, use about instead of on.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#about-on)

<a id="term-above"></a>
## `above`

**Scope:** `use-with-caution`  
**Rule:** Do not use for a range of version numbers. Prefer later. Do not use to refer to a position in a document. Prefer earlier or preceding. Do not use to refer to a position in the UI. Instead, write instructions that avoid directional language. Acceptable to use above in a non-directional way, such as when describing a hierarchy.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#above)

<a id="term-access"></a>
## `access (verb)`

**Scope:** `use-with-caution`  
**Rule:** Avoid when you can. Prefer friendlier words like see, edit, find, use, or view.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#access)

<a id="term-access-token"></a>
## `access token`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Lowercase except at the beginning of a sentence, heading, or list item.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#access-token)

<a id="term-account-name"></a>
## `account name`

**Scope:** `do-not-use`  
**Rule:** Do not use. Prefer username.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#account-name)

<a id="term-actionable"></a>
## `actionable`

**Scope:** `use-with-caution`  
**Rule:** Avoid unless it's the clearest and simplest phrasing for your audience. Omit it or prefer a phrase like that you can act on or useful. Do not use actionable in the legal sense without consulting a lawyer.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#actionable)

<a id="term-action-bar"></a>
## `action bar`

**Scope:** `do-not-use, android-only`  
**Rule:** In Android documentation, do not use. Prefer app bar.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#action-bar)

<a id="term-ad-tech"></a>
## `ad tech`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Write out on first mention: advertising technology (ad tech). Do not use adtech or ad-tech.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#ad-tech)

<a id="term-address-bar"></a>
## `address bar`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Use to refer to the URL bar or the combined URL bar and search box in a browser. Do not use omnibox.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#address-bar)

<a id="term-ad-hoc"></a>
## `ad hoc`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Acceptable in database and analytics contexts to mean "free-form" or "user-written" (for example, ad hoc queries or an ad hoc chart). For other contexts, try to find a more specific English equivalent. Do not hyphenate or italicize the term.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#ad-hoc)

<a id="term-admin"></a>
## `admin`

**Scope:** `do-not-use`  
**Rule:** Write out administrator unless it's the name of a UI label or other element. Acceptable to use admin in Android documentation.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#admin)

<a id="term-administrator"></a>
## `administrator`

**Scope:** `do-not-use, android-only`  
**Rule:** In Android documentation, do not use. Prefer admin.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#administrator)

<a id="term-advertised-route-priority"></a>
## `advertised route priority`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** Acceptable to also use base advertised route priority when discussing region-to-region costs. Do not shorten or use variations of these terms.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#advertised-route-priority)

<a id="term-agnostic"></a>
## `agnostic`

**Scope:** `do-not-use`  
**Rule:** Do not use. Prefer a more precise term like platform-independent.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#agnostic)

<a id="term-ai"></a>
## `AI`

**Scope:** `general`  
**Rule:** In general, you can use AI without spelling out artificial intelligence. Most readers are familiar with the abbreviation AI. If you think your audience isn't familiar with the term, spell it out on first use.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#ai)

<a id="term-aka"></a>
## `aka`

**Scope:** `do-not-use`  
**Rule:** Do not use. Instead, write out also known as, or present an alternative term using parentheses or the word or. You can also write out a definition.

[Official entry](https://developers.google.com/style/word-list#aka)
