# Numbers and units of measure

## GDSG-NUM-001 — Choose words or numerals consistently

- In ordinary prose, spell out zero through nine and use numerals for 10 and greater.
- Use numerals for exact technical values, measurements, dimensions, percentages,
  currency, versions, addresses, and other data where scanning matters.
- Spell out an ordinal in ordinary prose unless a technical label or compact display
  requires a numeral.
- Do not begin a sentence with a numeral; rewrite or spell it out.
- Prefer a decimal to a fraction when that form is more useful to the audience.

## GDSG-UNIT-001 — Join values and units correctly

- Put a nonbreaking space between a number and most unit names or symbols: `8&nbsp;GB`.
- Do not insert that space before a percent sign, currency symbol, or angular degree
  symbol: `25%`, `$40`, `90°`.
- Keep unit symbols unchanged after any number: `1 GB`, `10 GB`, not `10 GBs`.
- Spell out a unit when the symbol is unfamiliar or could be ambiguous.
- Follow the relevant scientific or engineering convention for capitalization.

## Ranges and compound units

- Use a hyphen without spaces for a compact range, or write **from X to Y**.
- Repeat a unit at both endpoints when omitting it could confuse the reader.
- Use a hyphen between multiplied abbreviated units when the publishing convention
  requires it; explain an uncommon compound unit.
- Distinguish decimal and binary data units accurately; do not treat `KB` and `KiB` as
  interchangeable.
- Use **k** for a compact thousands notation only in a context that defines and supports it.

When a number is hard to interpret, add a familiar comparison, implication, or visual—but
do not substitute the comparison for the exact value.
