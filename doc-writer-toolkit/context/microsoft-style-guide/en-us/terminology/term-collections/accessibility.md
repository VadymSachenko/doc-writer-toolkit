# Accessibility terms

- Default to people-first language, but honor known identity-first preferences.
- Name the specific disability or accessibility feature only when relevant.
- Avoid *normal*, *healthy*, or *able-bodied* as the contrast to disability.
- Avoid *special needs*, pitying language, euphemisms, and terms implying helplessness.
- Say *person who uses a wheelchair*, not *wheelchair-bound*.
- Use *blind* or *low vision* accurately; do not treat ordinary phrases such as *see*
  as automatically prohibited when they are natural and not exclusionary.
- Use *screen reader* as two words and describe compatibility precisely.
- Prefer the exact interaction or support need over a broad label.
