# Plain and precise English

- Prefer familiar, specific words over formal, abstract, or promotional wording.
- Do not assign a familiar word a new technical meaning when an established precise
  term exists.
- Use technical terms when the audience needs them; define unfamiliar terms at first use.
- Avoid internal jargon, fashionable business language, metaphors, and unexplained slang.
- Use one term consistently for one product concept.
- Prefer a positive instruction over a negative construction when both are accurate.
- Remove fillers such as *in order to*, *it should be noted that*, and *at this point in time*.
- Use US spelling in English content unless a product name, quotation, locale, or legal
  text requires another form.
- Avoid unnecessary Latin and non-English phrases when plain English communicates the meaning.
